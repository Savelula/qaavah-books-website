01

Open the shutters

Today's net, revenue against expenses, and the week beside the month. Anything that needs you — low stock, unpaid credit — is already at the top.

02

Ring up a sale

Tap a catalogue item, confirm the quantity, take the money. The line total does itself and the stock count drops by one.

03

Take it however they pay

Cash, MoMo, card, or on credit. Credit sales become a debtor automatically — nobody has to remember to write it down.

04

Watch the shelf, not the spreadsheet

Every sale moves the count. Items that drop below their reorder point flag themselves in amber before you run out mid-morning.

05

Chase what you're owed

Everyone who owes you, sorted by how long it's been. Past thirty days it turns red. One tap marks it paid and squares the books.

06

Cash up and close

Money in, money out, net for the day — grouped by category or listed newest first, and out as a CSV or PDF when someone needs to see it.

Why not the usual options

What a notebook, a spreadsheet and a POS all miss

Most shops end up running three systems that don't talk to each other. Each of these is a deliberate choice here, not a missing feature.

menu_bookA notebook forgets who owes you
check_circleCredit sales become debtors on their own

Take a sale on credit and it lands in Debtors with the date attached, ages itself, and turns red past thirty days. Mark paid closes it and squares the books in one tap.

table_chartA spreadsheet can't count stock
check_circleEvery sale moves the shelf count

Items carry a reorder point and flag themselves amber before you run out. Scan a barcode to find them, print a shelf label, and count without closing the shop.

point_of_saleA POS wants hardware and a contract
check_circleThe phone in your pocket, GH₵45 a month

No terminal, no card reader, no per-till fee. Add up to five staff on their own logins for a few dollars a seat, and drop them again when the season ends.

wifi_offCloud tools die with the connection
check_circleOffline-first, syncs when it's back

Sales save on the phone and post themselves when the signal returns, so a dead router doesn't stop trading. Accounting software just shows you a spinner.

Your people get logins, not your password

The moment someone else stands behind the counter, shared logins stop working. Every person gets their own account, sees only what their role allows, and leaves a trail you can read the next morning.

Owner
Everything
Reports, pricing, staff, plans. Sees every shop on the account.
Manager
Runs the floor
Sales, stock, debtors and reports for their shop — not billing or staff pay.
Cashier
Sales only
Rings up and records. Never sees margins, totals or anyone else's day.
  • check_circleFive seats included on Shop Pro, then GH₵10 a seat — add for the season, drop them after.
